1. Two layers: payoff vs pricing

Options Desk exposes two different questions. Mixing them up is the most common source of confusion.

Layer Question Depends on Ignores
v1 — Payoff at expiration If I hold to expiry and the stock finishes at $S$, what is my P/L? Strike, premium paid, contracts, fees, $S$ $r$, $q$, $\sigma$, path of the stock before expiry
v1.1 — BSM pricing What is a fair European price now, and how does it move if inputs move? Spot, strike, $T$ (from DTE), $r$, $q$, $\sigma$ Your fees, your scenario P/L chart (those stay on the payoff layer)
Rule of thumb: the main P/L chart answers “where do I make money at expiry?” The pricing panel answers “is the option rich/cheap under these assumptions, and what are my Greeks?”

2. Black–Scholes–Merton in this app

We price European calls and puts under the Black–Scholes–Merton (BSM) model with continuous dividend yield $q$. The underlying follows geometric Brownian motion with constant volatility $\sigma$. There is no early exercise.

Standard assumptions (know where the model is wrong):

  • Lognormal spot; constant $\sigma$ (no smile / skew).
  • Constant continuous rates $r$ and $q$; frictionless continuous trading.
  • No jumps, no stochastic rates, no borrow fees beyond $q$.
  • At $T = 0$, theo collapses to intrinsic (same as payoff layer).

Time to expiry in the app is $T = \mathrm{DTE}/365$ (ACT/365 style year fraction). DTE comes from expiration date minus today.

3. Pricing inputs (decimals)

All three pricing fields are entered as decimals, not percent points. Example: 5% → 0.05; 25% vol → 0.25.

3.1 Risk-free rate $r$

Continuous (or continuously compounded equivalent) funding rate used to discount the strike and to drift the forward. In practice quants often proxy $r$ with a short-term Treasury or SOFR-linked curve for the option’s tenor — not “your brokerage cash yield.”

  • Economic role: higher $r$ raises the forward $F = S e^{(r-q)T}$, which helps calls and hurts puts (all else equal).
  • UI: Risk-free rate (decimal), default 0.05.
  • Rho measures sensitivity of theo to $r$ (see Greeks).

3.2 Dividend yield $q$

Continuous dividend yield on the underlying. For an index this is often a blended yield; for a single stock it is a rough annualized estimate of expected dividends over the life of the option. Non-dividend stocks: use 0.

  • Economic role: dividends pull the forward down. Higher $q$ hurts calls and helps puts (opposite of $r$).
  • UI: Dividend yield (decimal), default 0.0.
  • Discrete cash dividends are not modeled explicitly — $q$ is the continuous proxy.

3.3 Volatility $\sigma$

Annualized standard deviation of log-returns assumed by BSM. In the desk you type an implied / assumed vol; the model does not calibrate to a market surface.

  • Scale: $\sigma = 0.20$ means 20% annualized. Daily move rough intuition: $\sigma / \sqrt{252}$ (about 1.26% for 20% vol) — order-of-magnitude only.
  • Dominant driver of time value for ATM options; vega is largest near ATM and longer $T$.
  • UI: Volatility σ (decimal), must be $\gt 0$ when $T > 0$.
Sign convention trap: entering 5 for “5% rate” or 25 for “25% vol” will explode the price. Always use decimals: 0.05, 0.25.

3.4 Related non-pricing inputs that still feed $T$

FieldRole in pricing
Expiration / DTE Sets $T$. $T=0$ ⇒ theo = intrinsic; Greeks mostly collapse.
Current stock price (spot $S$) Primary state variable for theo and Greeks; x-axis of theo-vs-spot chart.
Strike $K$ Defines moneyness; appears in $d_1,d_2$ and intrinsic floor.
Option cost / share Not an input to BSM. Used for edge = theo − market premium.

4. Core formulas

With $S$ spot, $K$ strike, $T$ years, rate $r$, yield $q$, vol $\sigma$:

$$ d_1 = \frac{\ln(S/K) + (r - q + \tfrac{1}{2}\sigma^2)T}{\sigma\sqrt{T}},\qquad d_2 = d_1 - \sigma\sqrt{T} $$ $$ C = S e^{-qT} N(d_1) - K e^{-rT} N(d_2) $$ $$ P = K e^{-rT} N(-d_2) - S e^{-qT} N(-d_1) $$

$N(\cdot)$ is the standard normal CDF. Put–call parity (European, continuous yield):

$$ C - P = S e^{-qT} - K e^{-rT} $$

Use parity as a unit-test sanity check when you change $r$ or $q$: the difference $C-P$ must track the forward discount factors, independent of $\sigma$.

5. Greeks — meaning and units (as implemented)

Greeks are partial derivatives of the theo price per share with respect to model inputs. Options Desk follows these conventions (see bsm_core.py):

Greek Definition App units Long call (typical) Long put (typical)
Delta $\Delta$ $\partial V / \partial S$ per $1 spot move $0 \to 1$ $-1 \to 0$
Gamma $\Gamma$ $\partial^2 V / \partial S^2$ delta change per $1 spot > 0 > 0
Theta $\Theta$ $\partial V / \partial t$ value per calendar day (annual/365) usually < 0 usually < 0*
Vega $\nu$ $\partial V / \partial \sigma$ per 1 vol point (e.g. 25% → 26%) > 0 > 0
Rho $\rho$ $\partial V / \partial r$ per 1 rate point (e.g. 5% → 6%) > 0 < 0

* Deep ITM European puts can show positive theta in some rate/dividend regimes; don’t treat “theta always decays long options” as a theorem.

5.1 How to read each Greek as a developer

  • Delta — hedge ratio in shares per option share (×100 for one contract). ATM call delta ≈ $e^{-qT}/2$ (near 0.5 if $q\approx 0$). ITM → toward 1; OTM → toward 0.
  • Gamma — curvature. Highest near ATM and short-dated. Large gamma means delta (and P/L) change fast when spot moves; hedging must rebalance more often.
  • Theta — clock decay of theo if nothing else moves. In the app, a theta of -0.05 means roughly −$0.05 per share per day of calendar time. Multiply by 100 for one contract.
  • Vega — response to implied vol. App scales to one percentage point of $\sigma$ (divide raw $\partial V/\partial\sigma$ by 100). Vega of 0.12 ≈ +$0.12 theo per share if vol rises from 25% to 26%.
  • Rho — rate sensitivity, also per one percentage point of $r$. Usually small for short-dated equity options; grows with $T$ and for deep ITM options.
Sign for long options: you are long gamma and long vega; you typically pay theta. Short options flip those signs. This desk only models long calls/puts.

6. How increasing one input affects another

Below: direction of change in European theo price when you increase one input, holding the others fixed. These are the comparative-static relationships a desk trader internalizes.

6.1 Effect on option price

Increase… Call price Put price Intuition
Spot $S$ ↑ ↓ Moneyness: calls benefit, puts suffer.
Strike $K$ ↓ ↑ Harder for calls to finish ITM; easier for puts.
Time $T$ (DTE) ↑* ↑* More optionality / variance to expiry (usual case).
Rate $r$ ↑ ↓ Forward $F=Se^{(r-q)T}$ rises → calls up, puts down.
Dividend $q$ ↓ ↑ Forward falls — mirror image of raising $r$.
Vol $\sigma$ ↑ ↑ Both benefit from wider terminal distribution (vega > 0).

* European deep ITM puts with high rates can violate naive “more time always helps”; rare in equity desk defaults but possible mathematically.

6.2 Cross-effects: changing one input reshapes Greeks

Greeks are not constant. Moving an input changes both the price and the Greek surface.

If you increase… What typically happens to Greeks
$\sigma$ (vol) Price ↑ (vega>0). ATM gamma usually ↓ (distribution spreads out; delta becomes less cliff-like). Vega often peaks ATM and can shift with vol level. Effective delta of OTM options increases toward 0.5 as vol rises (tails fatten).
$T$ (more DTE) Price ↑. Vega ↑ (more time for vol to matter). Gamma ↓ for fixed spot (gamma concentrates as expiry approaches). |Theta| per day often smaller when $T$ is large, then accelerates near expiry for ATM.
$S$ toward strike (→ ATM) Call delta → ~0.5; put delta → ~−0.5. Gamma and vega maximize near ATM. Deep ITM/OTM: gamma & vega → 0; delta → ±1 or 0.
$r$ Call rho > 0, put rho < 0. Mild effect on delta via forward (call deltas drift up slightly as $r$ rises, all else equal).
$q$ Opposite forward effect to $r$: call deltas drift down as $q$ rises (stock expected to lag the forward).

6.3 Coupled pairs to remember

  • $r$ vs $q$: only the difference $r - q$ enters the forward drift. Raising $r$ by 1% is directionally similar to cutting $q$ by 1% for the forward (discounting of strike vs spot still differs in the full formula).
  • $\sigma$ vs $T$: total variance to expiry is roughly $\sigma^2 T$. Doubling $\sigma$ is not the same as doubling $T$ for Greeks (gamma/vega surfaces differ), but both inflate time value.
  • Spot vs strike: moneyness $S/K$ (or log-moneyness) is the right state variable. Spot vs strike % in the UI is $(S-K)/K$.
  • Theta vs vega: long options are usually short theta / long vega — you bleed if realized vol is quiet; you gain if implied/realized expands.

7. Interpreting the graphs

7.1 Main chart — payoff at expiration

This is not a BSM chart. X-axis = stock price at expiry. Y-axis = dollar P/L or % return on debit.

  • Blue curve: long option P/L after premium + fees.
  • Green / red shading: profit vs loss zones relative to zero P/L.
  • Strike line: where intrinsic turns on (call) or off (put).
  • Break-even line: where P/L crosses zero (call: $K + D/M$; put: $K - D/M$).
  • Spot line: today’s price — reference only; payoff does not “know” spot except for markers / stock-compare overlay.
  • Scenario line: your what-if expiry price; drives the net-profit callout.
  • Stock overlay (optional): P/L of buying the same share count at today’s spot — shows leverage vs linear stock exposure.
Reading a long call: flat loss = −debit for $S \le K$; then rises with slope equal to shares (100 × contracts). Break-even sits to the right of strike by the per-share debit.
Reading a long put: flat loss = −debit for $S \ge K$; profit rises as $S$ falls, capped at $S=0$. Break-even sits to the left of strike.

7.2 Pricing chart — theoretical value vs spot

Shown when Show pricing panel is enabled. X-axis = underlying spot today (holding $T,\sigma,r,q,K$ fixed). Y-axis = BSM value per share.

  • Smooth curve: theo as a function of $S$. As $S\to\infty$, call ≈ forward-adjusted intrinsic; as $S\to 0$, call → 0. Puts mirror.
  • Slope of the curve ≈ delta at that spot; curvature ≈ gamma.
  • Horizontal market-premium line: what you typed as option cost / share. Theo above that line ⇒ positive edge (model-cheap to buy under your assumptions); below ⇒ model-rich.
  • Vertical strike / spot markers: orient moneyness on the theo surface.

What happens visually when you increase inputs on this chart:

  • ↑ $\sigma$: curve lifts (especially near ATM); becomes smoother / less kinked toward the intrinsic hockey stick.
  • ↑ $T$: similar lift and smoothing; approaches a more linear discounted payoff shape.
  • ↑ $r$: call curve shifts up; put curve shifts down.
  • ↑ $q$: call curve shifts down; put curve shifts up.
  • Move spot marker: you slide along the same theo curve; delta/gamma readouts update.

7.3 Do not confuse the two x-axes

ChartX meansY means
Payoff Terminal stock price at expiry Your P/L after debit
Theo vs spot Spot now (mark-to-model) Fair value per share under BSM

8. Theo, premium, and edge

$$ \mathrm{edge} = V_{\mathrm{BSM}}(S,K,T,r,q,\sigma) - \text{premium per share} $$
  • edge > 0: under your $(r,q,\sigma,T)$, the market premium looks cheap (theo higher than what you’d pay).
  • edge < 0: market looks rich vs your assumptions.
Edge is not alpha by itself. It only says the market disagrees with your vol/rate/dividend assumptions. If you understate vol, every option looks “cheap.” Treat edge as a consistency check, not a trade signal, until you trust $\sigma$.

9. Developer pitfalls & conventions

  1. Decimals vs percent: UI and SciPy path expect $\sigma=0.2$, not 20. Same for $r$ and $q$.
  2. Vega/rho scaling: raw calculus Greeks are per unit $\sigma$ or $r$ in decimal. Desk divides by 100 so “+1 vol point” matches trader speech.
  3. Theta per day: annual theta / 365. Not trading-day / 252. Document this if you compare to another library.
  4. $T=0$: price = intrinsic; gamma/vega/theta → 0; delta becomes a step (0 / 0.5 / 1 conventions at the strike).
  5. American options / early exercise: not modeled. Equity puts with dividends can be exercised early — BSM understates early-exercise premium.
  6. Fees and contracts: scale payoff P/L, not the per-share BSM price. Multiply theo × 100 × contracts when comparing to total debit.
  7. Put–call parity tests: best regression check when touching bsm_core. Finite-difference gamma checks catch PDF/CDF mistakes.
  8. Vol smile: one $\sigma$ for all strikes is wrong for real markets. Use this panel for intuition and scenario vol, not production marking.

10. Quick mental checklist

  • Payoff chart → expiry economics; ignores $r,q,\sigma$.
  • Pricing panel → mark-to-model; ignores your fees.
  • $r$ ↑ helps calls / hurts puts; $q$ ↑ does the opposite.
  • $\sigma$ ↑ lifts both calls and puts; watch vega and the flattening of gamma.
  • ATM: max gamma & vega; delta ≈ ±0.5.
  • Long option ≈ long vol, short time (usually).
  • Edge = model opinion − market premium; only as good as your $\sigma$.
  • Always enter rates and vol as decimals.
